Khayr, App & Feature Audit

A dua card deck in one self-contained file, now carrying two utility features: waktu solat for every JAKIM zone and a qibla compass that can calibrate itself from the sun. This audit reads the new build line by line, verifies its claims against the live JAKIM API and the mathematics, and names the four gaps between a working feature and a competitive one.

Audited 5 Aug 202660 JAKIM zonesBearing verified: KL 292.5°0 console errors
The verdict, in 60 seconds

Ship-grade engineering, hidden behind a three-dot menu.

The new build is honest work. Official times come straight from e-solat.gov.my for all 60 zones, a week at a time, cached for offline. Where there is no zone, a solar calculation takes over, with five methods and both Asr rulings. The qibla bearing is correct to the published figure, and when a device has no compass the feature says so and offers the sun instead of pretending. What remains is surface, not substance: the sheets speak English only, the reader must find their own zone in a list of 60, and both features live behind the overflow menu where no first-time reader will look.

Verified

The times are real

Live fetch against zone SGR01 and WLY01 returned official JAKIM times matching the API byte for byte, Hijri date included.

Verified

The maths is right

Great-circle bearing from Kuala Lumpur computes 292.5° from true north. JAKIM publishes 291° 7′ for the neighbouring zone reference point. Agreement within the expected margin.

Gap

Nobody will find it

Both features sit behind the ··· menu. Every competitor puts the next prayer on the first screen. Surface it, or it may as well not exist.

The product today
What one file contains

Khayr.html is the whole app. Thirty verified dua cards in four sets, three ways to hold the deck, English and Bahasa Melayu, saves with an honest free cap, a premium tier that only sells what exists, and a content pipeline that refuses hand-typed Arabic. The August update adds roughly 1,000 lines: a prayer-times sheet, a qibla compass with camera view, and the plumbing both need.

Offline continuityOne fetch stores a full week per zone. The sheet keeps working with no connection, and tops the week up when it can. A failed fetch backs off for 60 seconds instead of spinning.Verified
Anywhere else on earthWithout a zone, times are found by bisecting the solar altitude curve: one solar model serves both the compass and the clock, so the two can never disagree. Five methods, standard and Hanafi Asr. Polar edge case handled: an angle the sun never reaches reports itself instead of inventing a time.Verified
The countdownNext prayer with ring countdown, updated in place once a second so rebuilds cannot restart the animation. After Isyak it rolls to tomorrow's Subuh from the cached week. When the time arrives it says so for ninety seconds, then moves on.Verified
Clock disciplineThe Malaysian calendar day is computed at UTC+8 regardless of the device clock, and e-solat dates arrive in Malay: Ogos parses, and so does the English spelling. The Hijri date comes from JAKIM itself, 21 Safar 1448 today.Verified
App viewIn the phone layout the sheet takes its real phone shape, bottom-anchored at 392px. Zone choice carries across views.Verified
Why the JAKIM fetch matters: calculation and JAKIM agree within a minute or two on everything except Subuh, where the official table sits about ten minutes later. Apps that only calculate are quietly wrong in Malaysia five times a day. Khayr takes the official number and keeps the calculation as the fallback, which is the correct order.

04
The gaps
Four findings, none structural
#FindingRepairSeverity
1Both features live only behind the ··· overflow menu. The deck's readers will never discover them, and a prayer-times feature that is not glanceable has no reason to exist.A next-prayer chip on the masthead in both layouts: name, time, countdown. One tap opens the sheet.High
2The sheet chrome speaks English only. Prayer names carry BM inline, but Today, Next prayer, the zone notes and every button ignore the EN | BM toggle that the rest of the app honours.Route all sheet strings through the existing LANG switch. The pattern is already in the codebase.Medium
3Use my location does not pick the reader's JAKIM zone. It produces calculated times, while the official table sits one manual pick away in a list of 60.A static zone-to-coordinates table plus nearest-neighbour match, so location lands on the official times with consent to a rough guess.Medium
4No azan notification. A browser tab cannot reliably wake a phone, so the web version cannot honestly promise one.Do not fake it with unreliable web push. State it plainly, and carry it as the flagship reason for the native port.Noted
One dependency to watch: e-solat.gov.my is a single government endpoint with no SLA. The week cache absorbs short outages, and the calculation absorbs long ones, so the design already degrades gracefully. If it ever hardens its CORS policy, a small proxy becomes the fix.
05
Verdict
Where this leaves the product

The hard parts are done and done well: official data, honest fallbacks, correct mathematics, offline behaviour, accessibility scaffolding. The four gaps are all surface work, and all four are cheap relative to what was just built. Fix discoverability first: it converts an engineering achievement into a daily habit. Then BM, then the zone auto-pick. The notification question belongs to the native port and should be answered there, not faked here. The full sequencing lives in the Plan.
